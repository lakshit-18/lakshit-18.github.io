## Context

Checkout is the moment a B2B retailer's cart becomes a paid, fulfillable order. On our platform, one checkout touches five services: an API gateway that the mobile app talks to, a checkout service that orchestrates the flow, a payment service that owns payment sessions and expectations, a payment-gateway service that talks to PayU and Razorpay, and an order-processing service that reserves inventory and creates the order. Customers can pay fully upfront, pay a configurable advance, or pay on delivery, and can apply their wallet (JumboCash) balance on top.

## The problem

A checkout is a long, multi-step, cross-service transaction with real money in the middle. Any step can fail or time out: payment-session creation, the gateway round-trip, order placement, or the post-order bookkeeping. Two different actors can also try to finish the same checkout at once — the app returning from the payment screen, and the gateway's server-to-server webhook. Without care, that gives you the two worst outcomes in commerce: a customer charged twice, or charged with no order created.

## Constraints

- Every step must be safely retryable. Mobile networks drop, and users tap again.
- The flow has to support full payment, partial advance, zero advance (pay on delivery), and wallet + UPI split payments in one model.
- Business teams change prepayment policy often (by category, customer segment, cart value) and should not need a deploy to do it.
- The design must be debuggable after the fact: "what happened to this checkout?" needs a precise answer.

## Design

- **Explicit state machine.** A cart checkout moves through 11 states: initiated → payment session created → payment session successful → order placed → pending payment expectations created → completed. Each has failure and abort branches. An order-payment checkout (paying dues on an existing order) uses a smaller 7-state variant. Allowed transitions are table-driven, and every transition is appended to an **append-only transition log**, so a checkout's full history is always reconstructable.
- **Checkpointed snapshots.** Each significant step writes a JSON snapshot: cart details, payment options, the customer's payment selection, the payment-session initiation and completion responses, the order-placement response, post-advance payment expectations, and failure details. That is **9 checkpoint types**. When a request retries, the step reads its snapshot and resumes instead of redoing work. That is what makes retries safe.
- **Idempotent order creation.** Order placement writes a **DynamoDB conditional record keyed on the cart id with a 900-second TTL** before creating anything. A concurrent or repeated attempt sees the record and either replays the existing order or backs off. This collapses the app-callback vs webhook race into exactly one order.
- **Config-driven prepayment rules.** A rules table targets marketplace × customer segment × category × cart value, and resolves to an advance percentage. Conflicts resolve deterministically: a 0% rule wins, otherwise the highest percentage wins, and ties go to the newest rule. Policy changes are rows, not releases.
- **Split payments with per-item attribution.** A payment session holds one leg per instrument (UPI through the gateway, and the wallet). Payment expectations are created per cart item. An advance is allocated **pro rata** across items by what each still owes, so after the order is placed, every order item carries an exact balance due at delivery.
- **Zero-advance fast path.** When nothing is due upfront, checkout skips the gateway entirely and places the order directly.

## Key decisions & trade-offs

<Callout variant="tradeoff" title="Table-driven state machine over a workflow engine">A small transition table plus an append-only log was easier to reason about, test and operate than adopting a workflow framework. The trade-off: we own the rules, including making sure every failure state has a way out.</Callout>

<Callout variant="tradeoff" title="JSON snapshots over normalized tables">Snapshots made resume logic trivial and schema changes cheap. The trade-off is that snapshot shapes must stay backward-compatible while old checkouts are still in flight.</Callout>

<Callout variant="tradeoff" title="DynamoDB conditional write for order idempotency">A single conditional put is atomic and needs no lock service. The TTL bounds storage. The trade-off: a retry after the TTL window is no longer protected, so the window is sized well above the realistic retry horizon.</Callout>

## Results

- **~25K orders/month** through checkout at **99.57% payment-to-order completion** (payment-session completions that end in a placed order).
- **~50K checkout sessions/month** initiated at **98.4%** success.
- **~32K split-payment sessions/month** at **99.1%** success.
- Zero double-charges on retry by design: every step either resumes from its checkpoint or is blocked by the idempotency record.

## What I'd do next

- Add optimistic locking on the checkout row, so two concurrent completions are serialized by the database rather than by application checks.
- Write the transition and its snapshot in a single transaction.
- Move completion onto an outbox + worker model, so the app never waits on the gateway round-trip.
